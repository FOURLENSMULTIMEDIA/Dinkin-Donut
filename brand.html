01 · The idea

Old-school manners. Modern pickleball.

Dink Different borrows the quiet confidence of the old tennis clubs: whites on court, a handshake at the net, a members’ board on the wall. Then it opens the gate to everyone. It feels expensive without trying, warm rather than exclusive, and it never shouts.

Three words for every decision: Heritage. Warmth. Restraint.

03 · The mark

The Different D

Two D’s stand back to back, and one is flipped: that’s the different one. Together their bowls make a court seen from above, with the net in brass down the middle and a ball clearing it in a soft arc. That arc is the dink. Use the mark alone for buttons, embroidery and icons, the badge for kit and signage, and the wordmark for headers.

Leave clear space equal to the height of one D-bowl. The smallest size is 24 px on screen and 10 mm in print. Never recolour, stretch or outline the mark.

04 · Colour

Beige, navy & brass

Beige carries every page. Navy is for text and the deep panels. Brass is the only accent: rules, italics, buttons on navy. Ball yellow appears only on the ball. Oxblood is kept for losses and warnings.

05 · Type

Two typefaces

Display · Fraunces, soft
Aa Aa

A classy bold serif with soft ball terminals. Use weight 800 for headlines and the swash italic in brass for the second thought (“Play well. Dink different.”). Always set it with the soft axis on, large, with tight leading.

Text · DM Sans
Aa

For body copy, buttons and labels. Small labels are set in capitals with wide tracking (.3em), like the engraving on a trophy.

06 · Voice

How we speak

We say
  • “Play well. Dink different.”
  • “Whites welcome. Everyone welcome.”
  • “A handshake at the net.”
  • “See you on court.”
  • “Members”, “Dinkers”, “the club”
We avoid
  • Shouting, all caps headlines, exclamation marks
  • Slang, puns and emoji in headlines
  • “Exclusive” or “elite”: we are classic, not closed
  • Neon colours, gradients and stickers
  • Calling it tennis: it is pickleball

08 · Photography & kit

Real light, real texture

Golden-hour daylight, warm beige and cream tones, natural shadows and real materials: cable-knit, linen, cane, leather, canvas. Show people in whites and navy, paddles and a basket of balls. Never use stock-photo smiles, neon, or anything too clean. The site carries a fine paper grain so every page feels printed, not pixelated.
